### W1 — Gentle presentation

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Algebra / presentation and validation.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Definition 2.1, pp. 141–142.

**left**

Let $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$ be a quiver and $I$ an ideal of $\mathbb{k}Q$. The pair $(Q,I)$ is a gentle presentation if:

1. Each vertex is the source of at most two arrows and the target of at most two arrows.
2. For each arrow $a$, at most one arrow $b$ has $ab\notin I$, and at most one arrow $c$ has $ca\notin I$.
3. For each arrow $a$, at most one arrow $b$ has $t(a)=s(b)$ and $ba\in I$, and at most one arrow $c$ has $t(c)=s(a)$ and $ac\in I$.
4. The ideal $I$ is generated by paths of length $2$.

It is finite dimensional gentle if, in addition, there is an integer $N>0$ such that every path of length $N$ lies in $I$. An algebra is gentle, respectively finite dimensional gentle, if it is Morita equivalent to $\mathbb{k}Q/I$ for such a presentation.

**right**

Let $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$ be a quiver and $I$ an ideal of $\mathbb{k}Q$, with products in travel order. The pair $(Q,I)$ is a gentle presentation if:

1. Each vertex is the source of at most two arrows and the target of at most two arrows.
2. For each arrow $a$, at most one arrow $b$ has $ba\notin I$, and at most one arrow $c$ has $ac\notin I$.
3. For each arrow $a$, at most one arrow $b$ has $t(a)=s(b)$ and $ab\in I$, and at most one arrow $c$ has $t(c)=s(a)$ and $ca\in I$.
4. The ideal $I$ is generated by paths of length $2$.

It is finite dimensional gentle if, in addition, there is an integer $N>0$ such that every path of length $N$ lies in $I$. An algebra is gentle, respectively finite dimensional gentle, if it is Morita equivalent to $\mathbb{k}Q/I$ for such a presentation.

**In this application:** The input is a finite quiver with specified quadratic monomial zero relations. Validation checks the gentle conditions and rejects an oriented cycle of allowed successors, which would make the algebra infinite dimensional.

### W2 — Paths and the sets $\mathcal B$ and $\Gamma$

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Algebra / zero relations; Structure → Presentation / paths.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), §2, p. 143.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be a quadratic monomial algebra, with $I$ generated by a set $R$ of paths of length $2$. Let $\mathcal B$ be the paths having no subpath in $R$; their classes form a basis of $A$. Let $\Gamma$ be the paths all of whose length-$2$ subpaths lie in $R$. Write $\Gamma_m$ for the paths of length $m$ in $\Gamma$; in particular, $\Gamma_0=Q_0$ and $\Gamma_1=Q_1$. For $a_m\cdots a_1$, membership in $\Gamma$ means $a_{i+1}a_i\in R$ for $1\leq i<m$.

**right**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be a quadratic monomial algebra, with $I$ generated by a set $R$ of paths of length $2$ written in travel order. Let $\mathcal B$ be the paths having no subpath in $R$; their classes form a basis of $A$. Let $\Gamma$ be the paths all of whose length-$2$ subpaths lie in $R$. Write $\Gamma_m$ for the paths of length $m$ in $\Gamma$; in particular, $\Gamma_0=Q_0$ and $\Gamma_1=Q_1$. For $a_1\cdots a_m$, membership in $\Gamma$ means $a_i a_{i+1}\in R$ for $1\leq i<m$.

**In this application:** The path basis is $\mathcal B$, including the vertex idempotents. The smaller Bardzell complex uses $\Gamma_m$ in degree $m$.

### W3 — Path conventions

**Kind:** convention. **Shown:** Algebra / Paths.

**Source:** [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Conventions, p. 141; the application's own convention, [Methods, Conventions](../../docs/METHODOLOGY.md#conventions).

**left**

Paths are written from right to left, with left modules: an arrow $a$ followed by $b$ is written $ba$. Bar factors are listed in written order. Put $s_n=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}$, and let $R$ rewrite a path in the opposite order. The transport from travel order sends $[a_1|\cdots|a_n]\mapsto b$ to $s_n[R(a_n)|\cdots|R(a_1)]\mapsto R(b)$, and $a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n]$ to $s_nR(a_0)[R(a_n)|\cdots|R(a_1)]$. For $C_k$ in cyclic total degree $N$, use the sign $(-1)^{(N-k)/2+k(k-1)/2}$. Coordinates refer to these transported bases.

**right**

Paths and bar factors are written in travel order, with right modules: an arrow $a$ followed by $b$ is written $ab$. Put $s_n=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}$, and let $R$ rewrite a path in the opposite order. The transport from right-to-left notation sends $[a_1|\cdots|a_n]\mapsto b$ to $s_n[R(a_n)|\cdots|R(a_1)]\mapsto R(b)$, and $a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n]$ to $s_nR(a_0)[R(a_n)|\cdots|R(a_1)]$. For $C_k$ in cyclic total degree $N$, use the sign $(-1)^{(N-k)/2+k(k-1)/2}$. Coordinates refer to the travel-order bases.

**In this application:** Paths is a global display and input preference; changing it preserves the algebra and the coordinates of computed classes. Relations, typed presentations, representatives and exports follow this preference.

### O1 — The center

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Overview / The algebra / $HH^0(A)=Z(A)$.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Proposition 3.3, p. 146; $HH^0(A)=Z(A)$, the isolated-vertex case and the direct-product decomposition are standard.

**left**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a gentle presentation with $Q$ connected and having an arrow, and $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$. The space $HH^0(A)=Z(A)$ has basis the unit $1=\sum_{i\in Q_0}e_i$, the $\mathcal B$-maximal cycles $\alpha$, and the sums $\langle\langle\alpha\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\operatorname{rot}^i(\alpha)$ for one representative $\alpha$ of each cocomplete circuit, where $r$ is its period. Cocomplete means $\alpha^2\in\mathcal B$. For finite-dimensional $A$ there are no cocomplete circuits. For an isolated vertex the center is $\mathbb{k}$; for disconnected $Q$ take the direct product over its components.

**right**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a gentle presentation with $Q$ connected and having an arrow, and $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$, with products in travel order. The space $HH^0(A)=Z(A)$ has basis the unit $1=\sum_{i\in Q_0}e_i$, the $\mathcal B$-maximal cycles $\alpha$, and the sums $\langle\langle\alpha\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\operatorname{rot}^i(\alpha)$ for one representative $\alpha$ of each cocomplete circuit, where $r$ is its period. Cocomplete means $\alpha^2\in\mathcal B$. For finite-dimensional $A$ there are no cocomplete circuits. For an isolated vertex the center is $\mathbb{k}$; for disconnected $Q$ take the direct product over its components.

The isolated-vertex and direct-product statements are standard.

**In this application:** Overview displays the dimension of the center. Degree-zero Cohomology displays a computed basis as central elements; this basis need not be the basis in the proposition.

### O2 — Global dimension

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Overview / The algebra / Global dimension.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Corollary 3.14 and its proof, p. 160, with the Bardzell resolution (2), p. 143.

**left**

For a finite-dimensional gentle presentation $(Q,I)$ and $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$, the following are equivalent: $A$ has finite global dimension; $HH^m(A)=0$ for all sufficiently large $m$; there is no complete cycle in $(Q,I)$. In the finite case, $\operatorname{gl.dim}A=\max\{m:\Gamma_m\ne\varnothing\}$, equivalently the length of a longest $\Gamma$-maximal path. This equality follows by tensoring the Bardzell resolution with the simple modules: the differentials after applying $\operatorname{Hom}$ to a simple module vanish. An algebra supported on isolated vertices has global dimension $0$.

**right**

For a finite-dimensional gentle presentation $(Q,I)$ and $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ with products in travel order, the following are equivalent: $A$ has finite global dimension; $HH^m(A)=0$ for all sufficiently large $m$; there is no complete cycle in $(Q,I)$. In the finite case, $\operatorname{gl.dim}A=\max\{m:\Gamma_m\ne\varnothing\}$, equivalently the length of a longest $\Gamma$-maximal path. This equality follows from the right-module version of the Bardzell resolution of the simple modules, whose differentials after applying $\operatorname{Hom}$ to a simple module vanish. An algebra supported on isolated vertices has global dimension $0$.

**In this application:** The forbidden-successor graph determines the complete circuits and finite maximal paths. Overview reports infinity if a complete circuit occurs, and otherwise the maximum of those path lengths and $0$.

### O3 — Complete cycles and circuits

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Overview / Complete primitive circuits; Structure → Presentation / Complete primitive circuits.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Lemma 3.2 and surrounding definitions, p. 145; pp. 151, 157; §5.2, p. 188, for basic circuits.

**left**

A cycle is a positive-length path with equal source and target. For $C=c_m\cdots c_1$, put $\operatorname{rot}(C)=c_{m-1}\cdots c_1c_m$. A circuit is an equivalence class of cycles under rotation. Every cycle is uniquely $D^k$ for a primitive cycle $D$ and $k\geq1$; its period is $|D|$, equivalently the number of distinct rotations. A cycle is complete if $C^2\in\Gamma$; a circuit is complete if its cycles are complete. Let $\mathcal C(\Gamma)$ consist of complete circuits of even length, or of all complete circuits in characteristic $2$. A basic circuit in $\mathcal C(\Gamma)$ is not a proper power of another element of this set. Thus basic circuits are primitive complete circuits in characteristic $2$; otherwise they are the even-length primitive complete circuits and the squares of the odd-length primitive complete circuits.

**right**

A cycle is a positive-length path with equal source and target. In travel order, for $C=c_1\cdots c_m$, put $\operatorname{rot}(C)=c_mc_1\cdots c_{m-1}$. A circuit is an equivalence class of cycles under rotation. Every cycle is uniquely $D^k$ for a primitive cycle $D$ and $k\geq1$; its period is $|D|$, equivalently the number of distinct rotations. A cycle is complete if $C^2\in\Gamma$; a circuit is complete if its cycles are complete. Let $\mathcal C(\Gamma)$ consist of complete circuits of even length, or of all complete circuits in characteristic $2$. A basic circuit in $\mathcal C(\Gamma)$ is not a proper power of another element of this set. Thus basic circuits are primitive complete circuits in characteristic $2$; otherwise they are the even-length primitive complete circuits and the squares of the odd-length primitive complete circuits.

**In this application:** The circuit list contains one primitive complete cycle from each rotation class. Its length and the characteristic determine the repeating cohomology families; the list does not enumerate all powers.

### G1 — Hochschild cohomology and homology

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Groups → Cohomology and Homology / group heading.

**Source:** standard normalized $E$-relative Hochschild complexes, in the form used by the application.

**left**

For $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ finite dimensional gentle, put $E=\bigoplus_{v\in Q_0}\mathbb{k}e_v$ and $J=\operatorname{rad}A$. Tensor powers below are over $E$. The normalized relative cochains are $C^n(A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{E^e}(J^{\otimes_E n},A)$, with $J^{\otimes_E0}=E$. Chains are cyclic tensors $a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n]$, with $a_0\in A$ and $a_i\in J$ for $i>0$, that span $C_n(A)=A\otimes_{E^e}J^{\otimes_E n}$. Here $E^e=E\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}E^{\mathrm{op}}$; products and bar factors are in written right-to-left order. The differentials are

$(df)(a_1,\ldots,a_{n+1})=a_1f(a_2,\ldots,a_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^if(a_1,\ldots,a_ia_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_1,\ldots,a_n)a_{n+1}$,

$b(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n])=a_0a_1[a_2|\cdots|a_n]+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^ia_0[a_1|\cdots|a_ia_{i+1}|\cdots|a_n]+(-1)^na_na_0[a_1|\cdots|a_{n-1}]$.

Set $b=0$ in degree $0$. Then $HH^n(A)=\ker d^n/\operatorname{im}d^{n-1}$ and $HH_n(A)=\ker b_n/\operatorname{im}b_{n+1}$. A cochain $[a_1|\cdots|a_n]\mapsto b$ sends that tuple to the parallel path $b$ and all other basis tuples to zero.

**right**

For $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ finite dimensional gentle, put $E=\bigoplus_{v\in Q_0}\mathbb{k}e_v$ and $J=\operatorname{rad}A$. Tensor powers below are over $E$. The normalized relative cochains are $C^n(A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{E^e}(J^{\otimes_E n},A)$, with $J^{\otimes_E0}=E$. Chains are cyclic tensors $a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n]$, with $a_0\in A$ and $a_i\in J$ for $i>0$, that span $C_n(A)=A\otimes_{E^e}J^{\otimes_E n}$. Here $E^e=E\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}E^{\mathrm{op}}$; products and bar factors are in travel order. The differentials are

$(df)(a_1,\ldots,a_{n+1})=a_1f(a_2,\ldots,a_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^if(a_1,\ldots,a_ia_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_1,\ldots,a_n)a_{n+1}$,

$b(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n])=a_0a_1[a_2|\cdots|a_n]+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^ia_0[a_1|\cdots|a_ia_{i+1}|\cdots|a_n]+(-1)^na_na_0[a_1|\cdots|a_{n-1}]$.

Set $b=0$ in degree $0$. Then $HH^n(A)=\ker d^n/\operatorname{im}d^{n-1}$ and $HH_n(A)=\ker b_n/\operatorname{im}b_{n+1}$. A cochain $[a_1|\cdots|a_n]\mapsto b$ sends that tuple to the parallel path $b$ and all other basis tuples to zero. Representatives in the two conventions are related by the signed transport of W3.

**In this application:** Groups displays quotient bases found by exact linear algebra, together with cocycle or cycle representatives. A class's coordinates are relative to that computed basis, rather than to the families in G2 or G3.

### G2 — The cohomology of a gentle algebra

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Groups → Cohomology / group heading.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Theorem 3.12, pp. 158–159; notation from pp. 144–147, 151, 157; the isolated-vertex and componentwise extensions are standard.

**left**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a gentle presentation with $Q$ connected and having an arrow, let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$, and choose a spanning tree $T$ of the underlying graph. A pair $(\gamma,\alpha)\in\Gamma\parallel\mathcal B$ denotes the cochain sending $\gamma$ to the parallel path $\alpha$. Choose one representative of each complete and cocomplete circuit, compatibly with powers; $r$ denotes the period of the cycle in a sum. The following classes form a homogeneous basis of $HH^*(A)$:

| Family | Cocycles | Degree and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| $H_{\mathrm I}$ | $1=\sum_{v\in Q_0}(e_v,e_v)$ | $0$ |
| $H_{\mathrm {II}}$ | $(s(\alpha),\alpha)$ | $0$; $\alpha$ a $\mathcal B$-maximal cycle |
| $H_{\mathrm {III}}$ | $\langle\langle\alpha\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(s(\operatorname{rot}^i\alpha),\operatorname{rot}^i\alpha)$ | $0$; $\alpha$ a chosen cocomplete cycle |
| $H_{\mathrm {IV}}$ | $(c,c)$ | $1$; $c\in Q_1\setminus T$ |
| $H_{\mathrm V}$ | $(c,c\alpha)$ | $1$; $\alpha$ a chosen cocomplete cycle, $c$ its first traversed arrow |
| $H_{\mathrm {VI}}$ | $(\gamma,\alpha)$ | $\lvert\gamma\rvert\geq1$; $\gamma$ is $\Gamma$-maximal and the parallel paths $\gamma,\alpha$ neither begin nor end with the same arrow |
| $H_{\mathrm {VII}}$ | $\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{im}(\operatorname{rot}^i C,s(\operatorname{rot}^i C))$ | $m\geq1$; $C$ a chosen complete cycle of length $m$, with $m$ even or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$ |
| $H_{\mathrm {VIII}}$ | $(bC,b)$ | $m\geq2$; $C$ a chosen complete cycle of length $m-1$, $b$ its first traversed arrow, with $m$ odd or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$ |

In $H_{\mathrm {VII}}$ the signs $(-1)^{im}$ are all $1$ in the field under the stated hypothesis. For finite-dimensional $A$, families $H_{\mathrm {III}}$ and $H_{\mathrm V}$ are absent. For an isolated vertex there is only its degree-zero unit; disconnected algebras are treated componentwise (standard direct-product decomposition).

**right**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a gentle presentation with $Q$ connected and having an arrow, $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$, and $T$ a spanning tree. A pair $(\gamma,\alpha)\in\Gamma\parallel\mathcal B$ denotes the cochain sending $\gamma$ to the parallel path $\alpha$. All paths in this table are in travel order. Let $s_n=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}$, and define $\operatorname{rot}(c_1\cdots c_m)=c_mc_1\cdots c_{m-1}$. Choose one representative of each complete and cocomplete circuit, compatibly with powers. The following transported classes form a homogeneous basis of $HH^*(A)$:

| Family | Cocycles | Degree and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| $H_{\mathrm I}$ | $1=\sum_{v\in Q_0}(e_v,e_v)$ | $0$ |
| $H_{\mathrm {II}}$ | $(s(\alpha),\alpha)$ | $0$; $\alpha$ a $\mathcal B$-maximal cycle |
| $H_{\mathrm {III}}$ | $\langle\langle\alpha\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(s(\operatorname{rot}^i\alpha),\operatorname{rot}^i\alpha)$ | $0$; $\alpha$ a chosen cocomplete cycle of period $r$ |
| $H_{\mathrm {IV}}$ | $(c,c)$ | $1$; $c\in Q_1\setminus T$ |
| $H_{\mathrm V}$ | $(c,\alpha c)$ | $1$; $\alpha$ a chosen cocomplete cycle, $c$ its first traversed arrow |
| $H_{\mathrm {VI}}$ | $s_{\lvert\gamma\rvert}(\gamma,\alpha)$ | $\lvert\gamma\rvert\geq1$; $\gamma$ is $\Gamma$-maximal and the parallel paths $\gamma,\alpha$ neither begin nor end with the same arrow |
| $H_{\mathrm {VII}}$ | $s_m\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{im}(\operatorname{rot}^i C,s(\operatorname{rot}^i C))$ | $m\geq1$; $C$ a chosen complete cycle of length $m$ and period $r$, with $m$ even or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$ |
| $H_{\mathrm {VIII}}$ | $s_m(Cb,b)$ | $m\geq2$; $C$ a chosen complete cycle of length $m-1$, $b$ its first traversed arrow, with $m$ odd or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$ |

The pair notation incorporates reversal of the arrow bar factors. In $H_{\mathrm {VII}}$, $(-1)^{im}=1$ in the field, but $s_m$ need not be $1$. For finite-dimensional $A$, $H_{\mathrm {III}}$ and $H_{\mathrm V}$ are absent. An isolated vertex contributes only its degree-zero unit; disconnected algebras are treated componentwise.

**In this application:** The family table is computed from the presentation and the characteristic. The Cohomology view instead displays the row-reduced bar basis, so its class names do not identify these particular source representatives.

### G3 — The homology

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Groups → Homology / group heading.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Theorem 4.4, p. 169, and Corollary 4.5, p. 170, restricted to finite-dimensional gentle presentations.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle. In the Bardzell chain complex, $(\alpha,\gamma)$ denotes a chain with leading path $\alpha\in\mathcal B$ and arrow word $\gamma\in\Gamma$, with $\alpha\gamma$ cyclic. There is a degree-zero basis class $(e,e)$ for each vertex. For each complete circuit choose a cycle $C=c_m\cdots c_1$ of length $m$ and period $r$. If $(-1)^{(m+1)r}=1$ in $\mathbb{k}$, it contributes the two classes

$y_C=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{(m+1)i}(s(\operatorname{rot}^i C),\operatorname{rot}^i C)\in HH_m(A)$,

$x_C=(c_m,c_{m-1}\cdots c_1)\in HH_{m-1}(A)$.

For $m=1$, the second factor of $x_C$ is the vertex path. These classes together with the vertex classes form a basis. There are no cocomplete-circuit contributions for a finite-dimensional presentation.

**right**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle, with products in travel order. In the Bardzell chain complex, $(\alpha,\gamma)$ has leading path $\alpha\in\mathcal B$ and arrow word $\gamma\in\Gamma$, with $\alpha\gamma$ cyclic. There is a degree-zero basis class $(e,e)$ for each vertex. Choose a travel-order cycle $C=c_1\cdots c_m$ in each complete circuit, and set $\operatorname{rot}(C)=c_mc_1\cdots c_{m-1}$. Let $r$ be its period. If $(-1)^{(m+1)r}=1$ in $\mathbb{k}$, its transported basis classes are

$y_C=(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{(m+1)i}(s(\operatorname{rot}^i C),\operatorname{rot}^i C)\in HH_m(A)$,

$x_C=(-1)^{(m-1)(m-2)/2}(c_m,c_1\cdots c_{m-1})\in HH_{m-1}(A)$.

For $m=1$, the second factor of $x_C$ is the vertex path. The leading arrow remains the leading chain factor. These classes and the vertex classes form a basis; finite-dimensional presentations have no cocomplete-circuit contributions.

**In this application:** Homology displays a computed bar basis. The all-degree formulas count these circuit classes, including all powers of each primitive complete circuit satisfying the displayed sign condition.

**Source passages** are kept with the private working records.

### G4 — Cyclic homology and the Connes operator

**Kind:** construction. **Shown:** Groups → Cyclic / group heading and Connes and noncommutative de Rham homology.

**Source:** standard mixed-complex definitions; after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Proposition 4.11, p. 175, Propositions 4.12–4.13, p. 180, and Theorem 4.14, p. 181, in the finite-dimensional gentle case.

**left**

For the normalized relative chains of G1, with factors in written right-to-left order, the Connes operator is

$B(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n])=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{ni}1[a_i|\cdots|a_n|a_0|\cdots|a_{i-1}]$,

where the $i=0$ summand is $1[a_0|\cdots|a_n]$ and each bar factor is projected to $A/E=J$. It satisfies $B^2=0$ and $bB+Bb=0$. The cyclic complex has $CC_N(A)=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}C_{N-2j}(A)$ with differential $b+B$, keeping only summands in the target total degree. Its homology is $HC_N(A)$.

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle. On the basis of G3, $B(e,e)=0$, $B(y_C)=0$, and $B(x_C)=(m/r)y_C$, for a complete circuit of length $m$ and period $r$ satisfying $(-1)^{(m+1)r}=1$. The coefficient $m/r$ is the integer power exponent, interpreted in $\mathbb{k}$. Thus $H^{\mathrm{dR}}_n(A)=\ker B_n/\operatorname{im}B_{n-1}$ has the vertex classes in degree $0$, and both $x_C,y_C$ exactly when $m/r=0$ in $\mathbb{k}$. The cokernel of $B$ has the vertex classes, every $x_C$, and $y_C$ when $m/r=0$. The Connes spectral sequence degenerates on its second page, giving

$HC_N(A)\cong\operatorname{coker}B_{N-1}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\geq1}H^{\mathrm{dR}}_{N-2j}(A)$.

Negative-degree terms vanish and $B_{-1}=0$.

**right**

For the normalized relative chains of G1, with factors in travel order, the Connes operator is

$B(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n])=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{ni}1[a_i|\cdots|a_n|a_0|\cdots|a_{i-1}]$,

where the $i=0$ summand is $1[a_0|\cdots|a_n]$ and each bar factor is projected to $A/E=J$. It satisfies $B^2=0$ and $bB+Bb=0$. The cyclic complex has $CC_N(A)=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}C_{N-2j}(A)$ with differential $b+B$, keeping only summands in the target total degree. Its homology is $HC_N(A)$. Transport on its $C_{N-2j}$ summand is $(-1)^jT_{N-2j}$ as in W3.

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle. On the signed travel-order basis of G3, $B(e,e)=0$, $B(y_C)=0$, and $B(x_C)=(m/r)y_C$, for a complete circuit of length $m$ and period $r$ satisfying $(-1)^{(m+1)r}=1$. The coefficient $m/r$ is the integer power exponent, interpreted in $\mathbb{k}$. Thus $H^{\mathrm{dR}}_n(A)=\ker B_n/\operatorname{im}B_{n-1}$ has the vertex classes in degree $0$, and both $x_C,y_C$ exactly when $m/r=0$ in $\mathbb{k}$. The cokernel of $B$ has the vertex classes, every $x_C$, and $y_C$ when $m/r=0$. The Connes spectral sequence degenerates on its second page, giving

$HC_N(A)\cong\operatorname{coker}B_{N-1}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\geq1}H^{\mathrm{dR}}_{N-2j}(A)$.

Negative-degree terms vanish and $B_{-1}=0$.

**In this application:** Cyclic homology is computed from the total complex when its spaces fit the resource limits; the circuit formulas give dimensions in all degrees. Connes matrices and de Rham coordinates are displayed when both adjacent homology groups lie in the computed range.

### P2 — Cap product

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Operations → Products and operators / Cap product.

**Source:** standard normalized bar definition and the application's cap convention, Methods, Operations; after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), equation (44), p. 194, for the Bardzell-pair formula.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be gentle. The cap product is the right action $HH_n(A)\otimes HH^p(A)\to HH_{n-p}(A)$. On Bardzell pairs, for $(a,x)\in\mathcal B\odot\Gamma_n$ and $(y,b)\in\Gamma_p\parallel\mathcal B$, $(a,x)\cap(y,b)=(-1)^{(n+p)p}(ab,u)$ if $x=yu$, and is zero otherwise. **right**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be gentle. Products are in travel order and the action is written with the cohomology class first: $HH^p(A)\otimes HH_n(A)\to HH_{n-p}(A)$. The application uses $f\cap a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_n]=(-1)^{p(n+p)}a_0f(a_1,\ldots,a_p)[a_{p+1}|\cdots|a_n]$, and zero for $p>n$. For cocycles and cycles this represents the transported left-module action. Its literal signed transport on chains is the homologous suffix formula $f(a_{n-p+1},\ldots,a_n)a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_{n-p}]$.

**In this application:** The cap product takes a homology class and a cohomology class to $HH_{n-p}(A)$; the input order follows the Paths setting, and the result coordinates refer to the transported quotient basis.

### P3 — Gerstenhaber bracket

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Operations → Products and operators / Gerstenhaber bracket.

**Source:** standard normalized bar definition; after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Proposition 6.2, p. 214, for arrow derivations.

**left**

Let $f\in C^p(A)$ and $g\in C^q(A)$ be normalized cochains. In written right-to-left order,

$(f\circ g)(a_1,\ldots,a_{p+q-1})=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{(q-1)i}f(a_1,\ldots,a_i,g(a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{i+q}),a_{i+q+1},\ldots,a_{p+q-1})$,

$[f,g]=f\circ g-(-1)^{(p-1)(q-1)}g\circ f$.

Cochains vanish when a bar argument lies in $E$. This induces $HH^p(A)\otimes HH^q(A)\to HH^{p+q-1}(A)$. For a gentle presentation, an arrow $c$, and a Bardzell pair $v=(\gamma,\alpha)$, $[(c,c),v]=(\deg_c\alpha-\deg_c\gamma)v$, where $\deg_c$ counts occurrences of $c$.

**right**

Let $f\in C^p(A)$ and $g\in C^q(A)$ be normalized cochains. In travel order,

$(f\circ g)(a_1,\ldots,a_{p+q-1})=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{(q-1)i}f(a_1,\ldots,a_i,g(a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{i+q}),a_{i+q+1},\ldots,a_{p+q-1})$,

$[f,g]=f\circ g-(-1)^{(p-1)(q-1)}g\circ f$.

Cochains vanish when a bar argument lies in $E$. This induces $HH^p(A)\otimes HH^q(A)\to HH^{p+q-1}(A)$. For a gentle presentation, an arrow $c$, and a transported Bardzell pair $v=(\gamma,\alpha)$, $[(c,c),v]=(\deg_c\alpha-\deg_c\gamma)v$, where $\deg_c$ counts occurrences of $c$ in paths written in travel order.

**In this application:** Operations evaluates insertions on normalized bar representatives and projects the bracket to the quotient basis of its output group.

### P6 — Groups in higher degrees

**Kind:** construction. **Shown:** Operations → Higher degrees / Query a higher degree with the small resolution.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Bardzell resolution (2), p. 143, and Proposition 3.1, p. 144.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be a quadratic monomial algebra, $E=\mathbb{k}Q_0$, and $\Gamma_m$ the length-$m$ paths whose consecutive pairs belong to $I$. The Bardzell resolution has $\mathcal R_m=A\otimes_E\mathbb{k}\Gamma_m\otimes_E A$ and, for $m\geq1$ and $\gamma=a_m\cdots a_1$,

$d_m(1\otimes\gamma\otimes1)=a_m\otimes a_{m-1}\cdots a_1\otimes1+(-1)^m1\otimes a_m\cdots a_2\otimes a_1$.

$d^m(\gamma,\alpha)=\sum_b(b\gamma,b\alpha)-(-1)^m\sum_a(\gamma a,\alpha a)$;

**right**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be a quadratic monomial algebra, $E=\mathbb{k}Q_0$, and $\Gamma_m$ the length-$m$ paths whose consecutive pairs belong to $I$. In travel order, $\mathcal R_m=A\otimes_E\mathbb{k}\Gamma_m\otimes_E A$ and, for $m\geq1$ and $\gamma=a_1\cdots a_m$,

$d_m(1\otimes\gamma\otimes1)=a_1\otimes a_2\cdots a_m\otimes1+(-1)^m1\otimes a_1\cdots a_{m-1}\otimes a_m$.

Empty arrow products are the corresponding trivial paths; the augmentation sends $1\otimes e\otimes1$ to $e$. For a gentle presentation, $HH^*(A)$ is the cohomology of $\mathbb{k}(\Gamma\parallel\mathcal B)$, with

$d^m(\gamma,\alpha)=\sum_a(a\gamma,a\alpha)-(-1)^m\sum_b(\gamma b,\alpha b)$,

summing over arrows and including precisely the pairs in $\Gamma_{m+1}\parallel\mathcal B$. Tensoring $\mathcal R$ with $A$ over $A^e$ computes $HH_*(A)$. A degree-$m$ displayed pair is transported with coefficient $(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}$ as well as reversed path words.

### S1 — Maximal paths

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Structure → Presentation / Maximal permitted paths and Maximal finite forbidden paths.

For a positive-length path, maximality means that it cannot be extended by an arrow at either end while remaining in the same set. Paths are written in travel order. At an isolated vertex, the trivial path is both $\mathcal B$-maximal and $\Gamma$-maximal.

### S2 — Families in all degrees

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Structure → All degrees & ring / Homogeneous cohomology families.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Theorem 3.12, pp. 158–159, restricted to finite-dimensional gentle presentations; basic-cycle notation, p. 188.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle, and choose a spanning forest $T$. Families $H_{\mathrm I}$ and $H_{\mathrm {II}}$ consist of the component units and the classes $(s(\alpha),\alpha)$ for $\mathcal B$-maximal cycles, in degree $0$. Family $H_{\mathrm {IV}}$ consists of $(c,c)$ for $c\notin T$, in degree $1$. Family $H_{\mathrm {VI}}$ consists of the pairs $(\gamma,\alpha)\in\Gamma\parallel\mathcal B$ of positive degree $\lvert\gamma\rvert$, with $\gamma$ $\Gamma$-maximal and with distinct initial arrows and distinct terminal arrows whenever $\alpha$ has positive length. Families $H_{\mathrm {III}}$ and $H_{\mathrm V}$ are absent.

For each primitive complete circuit choose $D$ of length $r$. Set $d=r$ if $r$ is even or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$, and $d=2r$ otherwise. Put $C_k=D^{kd/r}$ for $k\geq1$, and let $b$ be the first traversed arrow of $D$. Its remaining families are

$H_{\mathrm {VII}}:\quad \langle\langle C_k\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(\operatorname{rot}^i C_k,s(\operatorname{rot}^i C_k)),\quad\deg=kd$,

$H_{\mathrm {VIII}}:\quad (bC_k,b),\quad\deg=kd+1$.

Together these families form a homogeneous basis, with only the unit for an isolated component. Componentwise use is the standard direct-product decomposition.

**right**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle, and choose a spanning forest $T$. Use travel-order paths and $s_n=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}$. Families $H_{\mathrm I}$ and $H_{\mathrm {II}}$ are the component units and $(s(\alpha),\alpha)$ for $\mathcal B$-maximal cycles, in degree $0$. Family $H_{\mathrm {IV}}$ consists of $(c,c)$ for $c\notin T$, in degree $1$. Family $H_{\mathrm {VI}}$ consists of $s_{\lvert\gamma\rvert}(\gamma,\alpha)$ with $(\gamma,\alpha)\in\Gamma\parallel\mathcal B$, of positive degree $\lvert\gamma\rvert$, with $\gamma$ $\Gamma$-maximal, $\alpha$ parallel to $\gamma$, and distinct initial and terminal arrows whenever $\alpha$ has positive length. Families $H_{\mathrm {III}}$ and $H_{\mathrm V}$ are absent.

For each primitive complete circuit choose its travel-order cycle $D$ of length $r$. Set $d=r$ if $r$ is even or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$, and $d=2r$ otherwise. Put $C_k=D^{kd/r}$ for $k\geq1$, let $b$ be the first traversed arrow of $D$, and let $\operatorname{rot}$ move the last arrow of a travel-order word to its front. The transported families are

$H_{\mathrm {VII}}:\quad s_{kd}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(\operatorname{rot}^i C_k,s(\operatorname{rot}^i C_k)),\quad\deg=kd$,

$H_{\mathrm {VIII}}:\quad s_{kd+1}(C_kb,b),\quad\deg=kd+1$.

These families form a homogeneous basis, with only the unit for an isolated component. Componentwise use is the standard direct-product decomposition.

**In this application:** Each row records a single class or an arithmetic progression of degrees, rather than a truncation at the current degree bound. The symbols for families are separate from the computed bar quotient-basis labels.

### S3 — Dimensions in every degree

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Structure → All degrees & ring / Query any degree from the formulas.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Corollary 3.16 and (20), p. 161, Theorem 3.12, pp. 158–159, Corollary 4.5, p. 170, Proposition 4.11, p. 175, and Theorem 4.14, p. 181; the formulas below count the listed basis elements.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle. The sequence $\dim HH^n(A)$ is bounded. If $N$ is the number of primitive complete cycles, then $\dim HH^n(A)\leq2N$ for $n>\lvert Q_1\rvert$. If $HH^n(A)\ne0$ for some $n>\lvert Q_1\rvert$, infinitely many cohomology groups are nonzero.

For every $n\geq0$, let $a_n$ count the degree-$n$ classes in $H_{\mathrm I},H_{\mathrm {II}},H_{\mathrm {IV}},H_{\mathrm {VI}}$, and let $c_n$ count complete circuits of length $n$ with $n$ even or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$, setting $c_n=0$ for $n\leq0$. Then $\dim HH^n(A)=a_n+c_n+c_{n-1}$.

Let $\delta_{u,v}$ be $1$ when $u=v$, and $0$ otherwise. For each primitive complete circuit $D$ of length $r$, sum over $k\geq1$ satisfying $(-1)^{(kr+1)r}=1$ in $\mathbb{k}$. Put

$h_n=\lvert Q_0\rvert\delta_{n,0}+\sum_{D,k}(\delta_{n,kr-1}+\delta_{n,kr})$,

$\beta_n=\sum_{D,k:\ k\ne0\text{ in }\mathbb{k}}\delta_{n,kr-1}$,

$e_n=h_n-\beta_n-\beta_{n-1}$.

Here $h_n=\dim HH_n(A)$, $\beta_n=\operatorname{rank}B_n$ and $e_n=\dim H^{\mathrm{dR}}_n(A)$. Values at negative indices are zero. Then $\dim HC_n(A)=h_n-\beta_{n-1}+\sum_{j\geq1}e_{n-2j}$.

**right**

Let $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$ be finite dimensional gentle, with paths in travel order. The sequence $\dim HH^n(A)$ is bounded. If $N$ is the number of primitive complete cycles, then $\dim HH^n(A)\leq2N$ for $n>\lvert Q_1\rvert$. If $HH^n(A)\ne0$ for some $n>\lvert Q_1\rvert$, infinitely many cohomology groups are nonzero.

Let $a_n$ count the transported degree-$n$ classes in $H_{\mathrm I},H_{\mathrm {II}},H_{\mathrm {IV}},H_{\mathrm {VI}}$, and let $c_n$ count complete circuits of length $n$ with $n$ even or $\operatorname{char}\mathbb{k}=2$, setting $c_n=0$ for $n\leq0$. Then $\dim HH^n(A)=a_n+c_n+c_{n-1}$.

Let $\delta_{u,v}$ be $1$ when $u=v$, and $0$ otherwise. For each primitive complete circuit $D$ of length $r$, sum over $k\geq1$ satisfying $(-1)^{(kr+1)r}=1$ in $\mathbb{k}$. Put

$h_n=\lvert Q_0\rvert\delta_{n,0}+\sum_{D,k}(\delta_{n,kr-1}+\delta_{n,kr})$,

$\beta_n=\sum_{D,k:\ k\ne0\text{ in }\mathbb{k}}\delta_{n,kr-1}$,

$e_n=h_n-\beta_n-\beta_{n-1}$.

Here $h_n=\dim HH_n(A)$, $\beta_n=\operatorname{rank}B_n$ and $e_n=\dim H^{\mathrm{dR}}_n(A)$ in the transported bases. Values at negative indices are zero. Then $\dim HC_n(A)=h_n-\beta_{n-1}+\sum_{j\geq1}e_{n-2j}$.

**In this application:** The query evaluates cohomology, homology, cyclic and de Rham dimensions and the Connes rank in any degree from $0$ to $100000$. It counts circuit families without constructing tensor spaces in that degree.

### S4 — The cohomology ring

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Structure → All degrees & ring / Full cohomology ring presentation.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Proposition 5.5, p. 189, Theorem 5.8, pp. 190–191, and Remark 5.9, p. 193; finite-dimensional case. Basic cycles are defined on p. 188; the isolated-vertex case and direct-product decomposition are standard.

**left**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a connected finite-dimensional gentle presentation and $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$. For an isolated vertex, $HH^*(A)=\mathbb{k}$ in degree zero. Otherwise choose a spanning tree $T$. Let $\overline{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{basic}}(\Gamma)$ consist of representatives up to rotation of complete cycles of even length, or of any length in characteristic $2$, which are not proper powers of another such cycle. A generating set $\mathcal G$ for the unital algebra $HH^*(A)$ consists of:

- $(s(\alpha),\alpha)$ for $\mathcal B$-maximal cycles $\alpha$;
- $(c,c)$ for $c\in Q_1\setminus T$;
- $(\gamma,\alpha)\in\Gamma\parallel\mathcal B$ with $\lvert\gamma\rvert\geq1$, $\gamma$ $\Gamma$-maximal, and the two paths neither beginning nor ending with the same arrow;
- $\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(\operatorname{rot}^i(C),s(\operatorname{rot}^i(C)))$ for $C\in\overline{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{basic}}(\Gamma)$ of period $r$.

Unless $Q$ has one vertex and one loop $a$ with $a^2\in I$ in characteristic $2$, $HH^*(A)$ is the free unital graded-commutative algebra on $\mathcal G$, modulo every quadratic monomial except $\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle^2$ and $(c,c)\smile\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle$ when $C$ passes through $c$, and modulo

$(c,c)\smile\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle=(d,d)\smile\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle$

whenever $c,d\in Q_1\setminus T$ both occur in $C$. In the excluded case, $HH^*(A)=\mathbb{k}[c,t]/(c^2)$, where $c=(s(a),a)$ has degree $0$ and $t=(a,s(a))$ has degree $1$.

**right**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a connected finite-dimensional gentle presentation and $A=\mathbb{k}Q/I$. For an isolated vertex, $HH^*(A)=\mathbb{k}$ in degree zero. Otherwise choose a spanning tree $T$. All paths are in travel order. Let $\overline{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{basic}}(\Gamma)$ consist of representatives up to rotation of complete cycles of even length, or of any length in characteristic $2$, which are not proper powers of another such cycle. Set $\operatorname{rot}(c_1\cdots c_m)=c_mc_1\cdots c_{m-1}$. The generating set consists of the transported classes:

- $(s(\alpha),\alpha)$ for $\mathcal B$-maximal cycles $\alpha$;
- $(c,c)$ for $c\in Q_1\setminus T$;
- $(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}(\gamma,\alpha)$ for $(\gamma,\alpha)\in\Gamma_m\parallel\mathcal B$, $m\geq1$, with $\gamma$ $\Gamma$-maximal and the two paths neither beginning nor ending with the same arrow;
- $t_C=(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(\operatorname{rot}^i(C),s(\operatorname{rot}^i(C)))$, for $C\in\overline{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{basic}}(\Gamma)$ of length $m$ and period $r$.

Unless $Q$ has one vertex and one loop $a$ with $a^2\in I$ in characteristic $2$, the ring is free unital graded-commutative on these generators, modulo every quadratic monomial except $t_C^2$ and $(c,c)\smile t_C$ when $C$ passes through $c$, and modulo $(c,c)\smile t_C=(d,d)\smile t_C$ when $c,d\in Q_1\setminus T$ both occur in $C$. In the excluded case, $HH^*(A)=\mathbb{k}[c,t]/(c^2)$, with $c=(s(a),a)$ in degree $0$ and $t=(a,s(a))$ in degree $1$.

**In this application:** All degrees & ring lists these generators, surviving quadratic monomials and identifications for each connected component; the ring for a disconnected algebra is their direct product. Its generator names are separate from the quotient-basis names in Operations.

### S5 — The cup product on the basis

**Kind:** theorem. **Shown:** Structure → All degrees & ring / Full cohomology ring presentation.

**Source:** after [CSSS26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.04.058), Table 1 and its entries (i), (ii), (iii), (v), (vi), pp. 184–187; finite-dimensional part. The one-vertex, one-arrow quiver is excluded from this table.

**left**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a connected finite-dimensional gentle presentation whose quiver is not one vertex with one arrow, and choose the basis of Theorem 3.12 and a spanning tree $T$. The unit acts as the identity. For complete cycles $C,D$ of even length, or of any length in characteristic $2$, write $t_C=\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle$ and $v_C=(bC,b)$, with $b$ the first traversed arrow of $C$. For $c\in Q_1\setminus T$, $(c,c)\smile t_C=v_C$ if $C$ passes through $c$, and is zero otherwise. **right**

Let $(Q,I)$ be a connected finite-dimensional gentle presentation whose quiver is not one vertex with one arrow, choose a spanning tree $T$, and use the transported basis of Theorem 3.12. The unit acts as the identity. In travel order put $\operatorname{rot}(c_1\cdots c_m)=c_mc_1\cdots c_{m-1}$. For a complete cycle $C$ of length $m$ and period $r$, with $m$ even or characteristic $2$, write

$t_C=(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(\operatorname{rot}^i(C),s(\operatorname{rot}^i(C)))$,

$v_C=(-1)^{m(m+1)/2}(Cb,b)$,

where $b$ is the first traversed arrow of $C$. For $c\in Q_1\setminus T$, $(c,c)\smile t_C=v_C$ if $C$ passes through $c$, and is zero otherwise. For powers $C,D$ of the same chosen primitive cycle, both of even length unless the characteristic is $2$, $t_C\smile t_D=t_{CD}$ and $v_C\smile t_D=v_{CD}$; otherwise both products are zero. All other products of two transported nonunit basis elements vanish; the opposite order follows from graded commutativity. An isolated vertex has only its unit.

**In this application:** The ring panel lists the generating products that survive these rules. Operations computes products in its bar quotient basis rather than identifying that basis with the families of Theorem 3.12.

### D4 — The Avella-Alaminos–Geiss invariant

**Kind:** construction. **Shown:** Surface / Avella-Alaminos–Geiss invariant.

**Source:** after [AG08](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2007.05.014), §2.2, p. 230; §3 and Remark 6, p. 232; Remark 8, p. 233; derived invariance: Theorem A, p. 229.

**left**

Let $A=\mathbb kQ/\langle P\rangle$ be a connected finite-dimensional gentle algebra with at least one arrow. A nontrivial permitted thread is a maximal path containing no relation. A nontrivial forbidden thread is a maximal sequence of pairwise distinct arrows $\alpha_n\cdots\alpha_1$ with $\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_i\in P$. At a vertex with at most one incoming and at most one outgoing arrow, include a trivial permitted thread $h_v$ provided that the incoming–outgoing composition, when present, is not in $P$, and a trivial forbidden thread $p_v$ provided that this composition, when present, is in $P$.

Choose $\sigma,\varepsilon:Q_1\to\{1,-1\}$ so distinct arrows with the same source have opposite $\sigma$, distinct arrows with the same target have opposite $\varepsilon$, and $\sigma(\gamma)=-\varepsilon(\beta)$ whenever $\gamma\beta\notin P$ is composable. For a nontrivial thread $H=\alpha_n\cdots\alpha_1$, set $\sigma(H)=\sigma(\alpha_1)$ and $\varepsilon(H)=\varepsilon(\alpha_n)$. At a trivial permitted thread set $\sigma(h_v)=-\varepsilon(h_v)=-\sigma(\gamma)$ if $\gamma$ starts at $v$, or $\sigma(h_v)=-\varepsilon(h_v)=\varepsilon(\beta)$ if $\beta$ ends there. At a trivial forbidden thread set $\sigma(p_v)=\varepsilon(p_v)=-\sigma(\gamma)$ or $-\varepsilon(\beta)$, respectively.

Starting with a permitted thread $H_0$, choose successively the forbidden thread $\Pi_i$ ending at $e(H_i)$ with $\varepsilon(\Pi_i)=-\varepsilon(H_i)$, and the permitted thread $H_{i+1}$ starting at $s(\Pi_i)$ with $\sigma(H_{i+1})=-\sigma(\Pi_i)$. Stop at the first return $H_n=H_0$ and record $(n,m)$, where $m=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ell(\Pi_i)$. Repeat until every permitted thread has occurred. Add $(0,m)$ for each primitive directed cycle of length $m$ all of whose consecutive arrow pairs are relations, counted once up to cyclic rotation. Then $\phi_A(n,m)$ is the multiplicity of $(n,m)$. It is a derived invariant and satisfies $\sum_{n,m}n\phi_A(n,m)=|\mathcal H_A|$ and $\sum_{n,m}m\phi_A(n,m)=|Q_1|$, where $\mathcal H_A$ is the set of permitted threads.

**right**

### D6 — Winding numbers

**Kind:** definition. **Shown:** Complete derived invariant / boundary table; Inspect cycle windings and intersection form.

**Source:** after [APS23](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00029-022-00822-x), Definition 3.1, p. 6; Definition 3.7, p. 8; §3.3 and Lemma 3.18, pp. 11–12; Proposition 3.20(5), p. 13; Theorem 4.3, p. 14.

**left**

A marked surface is a triple $(S,M,P)$ with $S$ an oriented open smooth surface, $M=M_\circ\cup M_\bullet$ a finite set of boundary marked points alternating in type on each boundary component, and $P=P_\circ\cup P_\bullet$ a finite set of punctures. Every boundary component contains a marked point; if the boundary is empty, both $P_\circ$ and $P_\bullet$ are nonempty. Finite-dimensional gentle algebras correspond to admissibly dissected marked surfaces with $P_\circ=\varnothing$.

A line field is a continuous section of the projectivized tangent bundle. For an admissible $\circ$-dissection $\Delta$ and its dual $\bullet$-dissection $\Delta^*$, the polygon construction of Lemma 3.16 defines, up to homotopy, a line field $\eta(\Delta^*)$ tangent to the dissection arcs. Let $\gamma$ be a smooth closed curve in $S\setminus P$, meeting the arcs of $\Delta^*$ orthogonally, such that each segment $\gamma_i$ between consecutive intersections is simple and lies in one polygon or punctured polygon $P_i$. Its winding number is $w^{\Delta^*}(\gamma)=\sum_i w_i$, where $w_i=1$ if the $\circ$ of $P_i$ lies to the left of the oriented segment $\gamma_i$, and $w_i=-1$ if it lies to the right.

If simple closed curves $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r$ bound a compact subsurface of genus $g'$ lying to the right of every $\gamma_i$, then $\sum_{i=1}^r w^{\Delta^*}(\gamma_i)=4-2r-4g'$. Applied to the boundary and puncture curves of a genus-$g$ surface, with boundary or puncture on their left, this gives $\sum_{j=1}^{b+p}w^{\Delta^*}(c_j)=4-4g-2(b+p)$.

### D7 — The complete derived invariant

**Source:** after [APS23](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00029-022-00822-x), Theorem 7.3, p. 23; Theorem 7.4, p. 24, and its proof, pp. 25–27; Lemma 7.5, p. 25; together with the boundary and puncture data of Remark 6.2, p. 21, which include the windings at punctures.

**left**

Write $g,b,p$ for genus, number of boundary components and number of punctures. Let $\mathcal B=\{c_1,\ldots,c_{b+p}\}$ consist of simple closed curves parallel to the boundaries and circling the punctures, with boundary or puncture on their left. Use primed notation for $A'$. Put $w=w^{\Delta^*}$ and $w'=w^{\Delta'^*}$. Let $n(j)$ count the $\circ$-marked points on boundary $j$, and set $n(j)=0$ for punctures.

The algebras are derived equivalent if and only if $g=g'$, $b=b'$, $|M|=|M'|$, $|P|=|P'|$, their pairs $(n(j),w(c_j))$ agree up to permutation for $1\le j\le b+p$, and the following genus-dependent condition holds.

- If $g=0$, there is no further condition.
- If $g=1$, the integers $\gcd\{w(\gamma),w(c)+2:\gamma\in\mathcal G,c\in\mathcal B\}$ and $\gcd\{w'(\gamma'),w'(c')+2:\gamma'\in\mathcal G',c'\in\mathcal B'\}$ agree.
- If $g\ge2$, one of the following three cases holds: each surface has an odd winding on its respective set $\mathcal G\cup\mathcal B$; all windings on both sets are even and some boundary or puncture winding is $0$ modulo $4$; or all windings on both sets are even, all boundary and puncture windings are $2$ modulo $4$, and

$\sum_{i=1}^g(\frac{w(\alpha_i)}2+1)(\frac{w(\beta_i)}2+1)=\sum_{i=1}^g(\frac{w'(\alpha'_i)}2+1)(\frac{w'(\beta'_i)}2+1)\pmod2$.

The genus-one gcds are therefore $0$ and $2$, so the algebras are not derived equivalent.

**In this application:** The example menu contains “APS §9 · Λ₁ (gcd 0)” and “APS §9 · Λ₂ (gcd 2)”. Their computed component signatures have the same boundary data and different gcds; the displayed ribbon-cycle basis need not be the source's $\alpha,\beta$ basis.

### D9 — Computing the complete derived invariant

**Kind:** procedure. **Shown:** Complete derived invariant / Inspect cycle windings and intersection form; Checks and conventions.

**Source:** the application's own procedure, docs/METHODOLOGY.md, “Complete derived invariant”; src/derived.js, connectedInvariant and symplecticReduction.

**left**

For each connected component, choose a spanning tree of the ribbon graph. Each remaining edge followed by the return path in the tree gives a fundamental cycle. At each turn, the contribution is $+1$ if the departing half-edge precedes the arriving half-edge in the thread order, and $-1$ otherwise; the winding is the sum of these turns. Contract the tree: the remaining half-edges form a cyclic word, and two fundamental cycles have intersection one modulo two precisely when their endpoints alternate.

Check that the number of cycles is $2g+b+p-1$, the intersection matrix has rank $2g$, and the boundary windings sum to $4-4g-2(b+p)$. In genus one, take the gcd of the nonseparating fundamental-cycle windings and the boundary values $w(c)+2$. In genus at least two, first detect odd windings among the fundamental cycles and boundary curves, then a boundary winding congruent to zero modulo four. In the remaining case, set $q(\gamma)=w(\gamma)/2+1$ modulo two on the fundamental cycles, extend by $q(x+y)=q(x)+q(y)+x\cdot y$, check that $q$ vanishes on the radical of the intersection form, and compute the Arf invariant by symplectic elimination. Repeat with the reverse edge order when choosing a spanning tree and compare the component signatures.

Displayed thread orders and edge walks follow right-to-left path notation; the winding values use the APS convention.
